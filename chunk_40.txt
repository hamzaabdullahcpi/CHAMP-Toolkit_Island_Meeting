InfraCredit Nigeria
 is a strong example of how a local credit-enhancement facility can unlock domestic capital for infrastructure. Established in 2017 through a partnership between PIDG
, GuarantCo
 and the Nigeria Sovereign Investment Authority, InfraCredit provides NGN-
denominated guarantees that improve the credit quality of local-currency debt instruments and help projects access longer-tenor finance from institutional investors such as pension funds, insurers and banks
 that would otherwise 
not invest
. By end-2024, it had guaranteed 24 transactions mobilising over NGN 200 billion in private investment, supported nearly NGN 150 billion in bond issuances, and helped first-time issuers such as North South Power access the domestic capital market. 
InfraCredit’s portfolio spans projects in Abuja, Lagos, Ondo, Imo, Abia, Cross River, Enugu, Bayelsa, Ado Ekiti and Victoria Island, including solar mini-grids, embedded power, housing, telecoms, a ring road and industrial infrastructure, showing how a national credit-enhancement vehicle can channel finance into a wide range of place-based investments.
The model has also been replicated beyond Nigeria through sister facilities in Pakistan, Kenya and Cambodia, with additional markets including Vietnam, Ghana and Francophone West Africa under development or review.

Kommuninvest 
is a
 locally led 
Swedish 
municipal aggregation 
mechanism
 that pools borrowing demand from municipalities and regions, giving them access to capital on more favourable terms. It raises money in the market and on-lends it to its members, operating as a lending-only institution rather than a grant fund or equity vehicle. The model is backed by a joint guarantee from its 282 municipal and 15 regional members, which helps de-risk lending and lower borrowing costs. In 2025, Kommuninvest’s total lending reached SEK 563.9 billion, showing the scale that pooled municipal finance can achieve.
