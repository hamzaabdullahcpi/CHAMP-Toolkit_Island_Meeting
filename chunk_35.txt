Tamil Nadu Water and Sanitation Pooled Fund
 illustrates how a dedicated special purpose vehicle can connect municipal projects with state, national and private finance. Established by the Government of Tamil Nadu as a separate trust, the Fund pooled the borrowing needs of 13 small and medium-sized municipalities and issued a single bond to raise long-term capital for water and sanitation investments. Participating municipalities repaid the Fund through project and local revenues, while the state government strengthened the structure through reserve funds and the ability to intercept fiscal transfers if repayments fell short; a USAID partial guarantee provided additional protection to investors.
 
 By ring-fencing the financing and repayment arrangements, the SPV spread risk, reduced transaction costs and enabled municipalities that could not issue bonds independently to access capital markets. 
The national government 
subsequently embedded this approach in its national 
Pooled Finance Development Fund
, through which the central government 
supports
 state-level pooled finance entities with project-preparation assistance and credit enhancement. The case shows how national and state governments can establish an intermediary SPV that aggregates local investment needs and mobilizes private capital while municipalities retain responsibility for project delivery and repayment.
