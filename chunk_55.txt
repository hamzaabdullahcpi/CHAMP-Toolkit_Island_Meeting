Intra-city aggregated procurement
 in Zurich demonstrates how a city government can leverage its regulatory mandates 
and purchasing power to aggregate demand from public and private sectors within its jurisdiction and create 
a market for low-carbon infrastructure. 
In 2002, the
 city began with a pilot showing that a new school could use 80 percent recycled aggregates in concrete, then introduced a 25 percent minimum requirement for recycled aggregates in concrete for new public buildings in 
2005 and
 later tightened its procurement in 2013 to require low-carbon cement as well. Over time, this public procurement approach, together with revisions to Swiss standards and active engagement with local producers, helped create a local market for recycled concrete that later spread into the private sector.
 
As a result, 90% of public concrete
 used in the city
 is now recycled (up to 50% recycled aggregates), and 
between 2016-21, the city’s 
concrete-related 
CO
₂
 emissions were reduced by 25
–
30% (7,430 tonnes CO
₂
 emissions saved). 
Zurich’s 
procurement policy succeeded in creating a market for recycled con
crete that was later spread to the private market, helping aggregate 
the city-wide demand towards low-emissions infrastructure.
