Arrange the financing structure in a way that the government backs the investment, while banks, 
institutional investors
, corporates or other investors provide the finance
 and technical assistance and additional de-risking where needed.
 
Develop long-term partnerships with
 
donors, governments, DFIs and investors to continually capitalize the investment vehicle/structure based on the risks, returns and investor appetite.
Illustrative examples
Kenya’s County Climate Change Funds (CCCFs) 
demonstrate how devolved climate finance can enable locally led adaptation while remaining anchored in national policy. 
CCCFs were piloted in Isiolo in 2011–12 and later expanded to Garissa, Kitui, Makueni, Wajir and beyond.
 
These funds
 identify, prioritize and finance local adaptation investments through county planning and budgeting systems, with county and ward committees steering decisions and local knowledge and climate information built into planning and monitoring. 
They are designed to blend resources from international climate finance, multilateral development banks, the private sector, national government transfers and county own-source budgets, and several counties have legislated commitments of 1–2 per cent of development budgets to capitalise the fund. This domestic commitment helped attract external 
finance
, including seed funding from the UK and Sweden and a proposal to the Green Climate Fund to expand the model.
 
The model is aligned with Kenya’s 
National Adaptation Plan (NAP), the National Climate Change Action Plan, and the Climate Change Act
.
