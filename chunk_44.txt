 The portfolio should connect governance, finance, procurement, regulation, behaviour and technology so that the combined effect shifts the system, not just individual parts of it.
 
Not every intervention needs to be commercially investible; the portfolio can generate investible pipelines as learning progresses.
Use implementation to generate evidence that “de-risks” policy and investment and enables scale-up.
 Track what changes in regulation, costs, technical performance, institutional arrangements, behaviour and business models—not simply whether an individual technology works.
Illustrative examples
System Demonstrators
 
provide implementation environments that put investible portfolios into on-ground local testbeds where public, private and civic actors jointly shape the implementation, exchange learnings and
 adapt as needed. Rather than a project-by-project implementation approach, the demonstrators 
create a systemic implementation environment 
around a shared mission and a coordinated portfolio of investments, with continuous orchestration around governance, finance, 
procurement, infrastructure, regulations, behaviors and technology. 
Key examples of System Demonstrator implementation are highlighted below: 
CoAction Lund
 
shows how a city-led system demonstrator can orchestrate a complex implementation ecosystem around a shared mission of climate neutrality by 2030. In Brunnshög, the municipality brought together infrastructure providers, employers, property owners, universities and solution providers to deliver more than 40 coordinated activities across mobility and energy, with funding kept flexible so that resources can shift toward the most promising models as learning emerges. One major “game changer” is 
EnergyNet
, a decentralised local electricity network that enables buildings, vehicles and local energy resources to share renewable electricity
. 
 
By
 testing 
EnergyNet on ground
, CoAction Lund has surfaced regulatory and market barriers around grid codes, data-sharing and local markets, helping inform the adjustments needed for 
scale and mainstream 
distributed energy systems
 in cities facing increasing electrification demands and grid constraints
.
