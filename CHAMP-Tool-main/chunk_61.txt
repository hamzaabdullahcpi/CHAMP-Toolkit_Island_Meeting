How to enable partnerships: 
To enable such partnerships, national, subnational or local governments can undertake the following actions: 
Start with the implementation bottleneck.
 Clarify whether the main constraint is capacity, project preparation, de-risking, financing, or delivery support; that determines whether the right entry point is a city network, an MDB/DFI, a climate fund, a technical assistance facility, or a philanthropic partner.
Based on the support required, identify the key partner institution.
 
Early engagement typically 
requires
 knowledge exchange and capacity building; program readiness requires project preparation support; investment programming needs grants 
and technical assistance
 TA; financing needs blended or concessional instruments and guarantees; and implementation needs ongoing coordination, learning and delivery support.
Bring in the relevant public counterpart early.
 For partnerships with MDBs and DFIs, connect through finance ministries, planning ministries or the national platform secretariat so the proposal fits country priorities, governance arrangements and the partner’s operating criteria.
Use intermediaries to connect cities to support.
 City networks, national task forces, subnational technical working groups and country-platform secretariats can help broker access to finance institutions, TA providers and climate funds, especially where cities cannot engage directly.
 
Prepare a clear concept note or project brief 
that sets out the urban priority, the delivery gap, the support needed, and how finance, TA and implementation roles will be combined across actors.
Part II: COUNTRY MULTILEVEL GOVERNANCE JOURNEYS
This part of the Toolkit will complement Part I by showcasing how different multilevel governance ‘Actions’ and their ‘Action Pathways’ interact in practice across country contexts. 
The Country Journeys will include deep dive analysis grounded in real-world contexts, showcasing institutional relationships, learning loops, implementation cycles, accountability mechanisms, and enabling conditions required to implement and scale Action Pathways across different countries. 
The Country Journeys will also seek to identify institutional friction points, governance bottlenecks along with challenges and lessons learnt from implementation experiences. 
Country Journeys are intended not only to document implementation approaches, but also to showcase how cities, regions and national governments have co-developed practical solutions that can inform other CHAMP countries.
This part of the Toolkit will have a special focus on the interactions between institutions and programs, governance capabilities, intermediary functions, financing and implementation platforms, and feedback loops required to sustain long-term climate investment.
