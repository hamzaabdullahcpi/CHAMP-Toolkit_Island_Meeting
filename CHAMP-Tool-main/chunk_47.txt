CCFLA’s Brazil Local Hub
 works as a place-based implementation platform that brings together 50 local
, national 
and global partners to help cities facing floods, droughts and infrastructure gaps move from fragmented demand to more coordinated urban climate action. Rather than operating as a stand-alone convening effort, it is built around two implementation-oriented initiatives: a community-of-practice that aligns actors and accelerates implementation, and the PCVR Project Bank, co-developed with Brazil’s
 
Ministry of the Environment, which maps subnational urban projects at different stages so they can be matched more effectively with technical assistance, funding and finance
.

CITIIS 2.0 (City Investments to Innovate, Integrate and Sustain)
 in India shows how a national programme can create a collaborative portfolio environment that helps cities to implement their climate-related project portfolios. 
Conceived by the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs with the French Development Agency, KfW, the European Union and 
the National Institute of Urban Affairs (NIUA)
, the programme supports up to 18 competitively selected city projects focused on circular economy and integrated waste management, while also backing state-level climate action and national-level institutional strengthening. The programme is coordinated through a PMU at NIUA, which the operational guidelines say is responsible for overall management and support on project management, environmental and social safeguards, monitoring and evaluation, knowledge capitalisation, and communications and outreach
. S
elected cities are supported by domestic experts and a pool of international mentors and transversal experts, with domestic experts providing day-to-day assistance and regular on-site missions. Governance 
of CITIIS is 
reinforced through a jury and an Apex Committee for final selection and periodic review, helping connect funding decisions to delivery capacity and accountability.
