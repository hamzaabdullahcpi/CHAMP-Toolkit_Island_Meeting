Colombia's 
 
Strategic Committee on Cities and Climate Change
 illustrates how an overarching, iterative coordination framework can support long-term multilevel climate governance. Co-hosted by the national government and WRI, the committee is not a one-off convening but the institutional architecture through which national authorities, subnational and city governments, and partners such as NGOs and funders coordinate climate action and investment over time. It operates through recurring cycles: a biannual forum brings these actors together to review city climate plans for alignment with national commitments such as Colombia's updated NDC and long-term strategy (E2050), identify active projects and synergies, prioritise high-impact and feasible measures, and connect them to enabling mechanisms — for example the Corridor of Climate Financing, a platform led by the National Planning Department that links climate projects to funders and provides support. By convening the same actors repeatedly rather than through one-off consultations, the framework builds long-term institutional relationships, strengthens implementation capacity and creates continuous feedback loops between planning, financing and delivery, helping the country address persistent political, financial and technical barriers and sustain its climate transition as circumstances evolve.
