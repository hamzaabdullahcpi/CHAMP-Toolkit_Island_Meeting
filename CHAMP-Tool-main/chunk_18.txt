Morocco's 
 
4C Maroc (Competence Centre for Climate Change) demonstrates how a dedicated intermediary unit constituted as an independent competence centre — rather than a line directorate within a ministry — can strengthen climate governance by acting as a neutral, multi-stakeholder platform. Formally established in 2016 as a public-interest group, and with a membership organised into four "colleges" spanning public institutions and local authorities, the private sector, research and training bodies, and civil society, it performs three complementary activities:
Strengthening the capacities of national actors across the climate agenda,
Capitalising knowledge on vulnerability, adaptation, mitigation and climate finance, and developing decision-support tools such as climate databases and vulnerability indicators,
Convening dialogue and coordination among diverse stakeholders while supporting the national greenhouse-gas monitoring system that underpins Morocco's UNFCCC reporting.
Through these activities, 4C Maroc performs the convening, facilitation, capacity-building and monitoring functions of an intermediary unit while remaining institutionally distinct from any single ministry, extending its reach through regional and pan-African outreach.
