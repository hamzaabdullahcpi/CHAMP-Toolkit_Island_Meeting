Overview
What it is: 
A collaborative portfolio implementation environment is a place-based setup where public, private and civic actors jointly implement, test and adapt a portfolio of mutually reinforcing interventions around a shared transition mission
.
 
These place-based initiatives go beyond 
individual pilots or project-level coordination activities. Instead, 
they
 create a shared, ongoing environment where governance, finance, procurement, regulations
, organizational cultures 
and technologies are aligned around a common mission
, such as 
climate action.
 
These implementation environments help generate the evidence, partnerships and institutional changes needed to move successful interventions toward investment and scale.
Why it is important
: 
Developing a portfolio and the governance and finance arrangements around it is not sufficient on its own; climate portfolios also need collaborative implementation environments where public, private and civic actors can joint
ly contribute, deliver, learn and adapt
. Without that environment, portfolios can fragment into isolated projects, learning is harder to share, and the system conditions needed for scale do not change.
Key actors: 
City and subnational governments, relevant national ministries and agencies, utilities and infrastructure owners, property owners and developers, businesses and solution providers, financiers and investors, procurement actors, 
academia
 
and
 civil-society
Implementation guidance
The below actions could help create collaborative portfolio environments
. 
Anchor implementation in a locally relevant mission and system boundary.
 Connect it to the city’s climate/investment priorities and, where possible, national climate priorities, rather than starting with a technology or individual project.
Map the system, actors, structural barriers and opportunities before selecting solutions.
 This is important because the system demonstrator experience showed that starting with existing solutions often reproduces incremental pilots rather than creating space for more transformative approaches.
Start with a real local testbed where the portfolio can be implemented against actual users, assets and constraints.
 It should be bounded enough to manage but expansive enough to generate evidence for scale and expose the governance, infrastructure or market challenges to overcome. 
Convene the delivery ecosystem and keep it aligned through continuous orchestration.
