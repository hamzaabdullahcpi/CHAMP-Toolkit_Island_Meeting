The demonstrator’s key move was site aggregation: by treating many small council-owned sites as one programme, Bristol created the scale needed for delivery, prompted the council to review its treasury strategy to enable programme-level appraisal, and secured a development agreement of around £10 million through an existing national procurement framework. The model then fed into the UK government’s Small Sites Aggregator, a national programme launched by the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government with private and public partners and an ambition of enabling 10,000 social rent homes a year.

Green Routes, 
Bogotá
 demonstrates how a system demonstrator can build a collaborative implementation environment around a shared evidence base. In Bogotá, the challenge was to coordinate thousands of freight operators in a citywide logistics system, so the demonstrator brought freight operators, shippers and public agencies together through the Urban Logistics Network, led by the city’s Secretariat of Mobility and working with the Secretariat of Environment. 
As part of the implementation, Green Routes, a
 consortium 
of four technology companies, 
developed a real-time routing platform
, which is part of an interconnected portfolio or related actions, including
 emissions labelling, circulation rules, loading and unloading investments, sustainable driving guidelines and a proposed district freight fund. 
The platform’s
 key contribution is the negotiated data-sharing arrangement, which would give the city the evidence it needs to plan freight more effectively, while also helping operators optimise routes and costs.
